Method and sources

Demand. Output × electricity intensity of the net-zero route (Vogl et al. 2018 for hydrogen DRI; electrolysis at ~53 kWh/kg H₂). Each route has a flexible share, the part that runs through electrolysers and can follow the weather if hydrogen can be stored.

Local mix. For each industry and region, a linear programme picks the cheapest capacities of solar, onshore and offshore wind, 4-hour batteries, extra electrolyser and hydrogen storage, SMRs, gas with carbon capture and capped gas backup to serve the plant's load. The year is cut into eight slices: winter or summer, windy or calm, day or night. Each slice's output is derived from the region's average capacity factors with fixed shape rules: solar seasonality grows with latitude, calm spells run at a quarter of average wind, and batteries shift sun into the night. Hydrogen storage is cheap in salt-cavern regions and dear elsewhere. If a region's land for wind and solar runs out, the remaining load falls back to its no-renewables mix.

Planner. Plants are indivisible and keep their size. The planner minimises total power cost by moving whole plants between regions, sharing each region's renewable potential among the plants it hosts (greedy construction plus local search). First best uses land and weather only. Second best adds the ticked constraints. Stay put keeps every plant where it is, each with its own best local mix. Product transport and the cost of moving process kit are left out; set a relocation premium under Assumptions to price them in.

Costs. Battery packs fell to $70/kWh for stationary storage in 2025 and European turnkey systems cost $275/kWh in 2024 (BNEF); electrolysers made outside China cost $1,900–2,500/kW installed (IEA, 2026); salt-cavern storage costs €0.66–1.75/kg (EWI); SMR target costs run €52–119/MWh (Arthur D. Little). Italy legalised SMRs in September 2026 (BGEN); bans or phase-outs remain in Austria, Germany, Denmark, Spain, Ireland, Luxembourg and Portugal.
